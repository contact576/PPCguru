## How an HVAC Company Scaled to ~396 Meta Ads Leads a Month

**Headline results:** ~396 Leads / month · ~$26 Cost per lead · ~12× Lead growth since launch

PPC Guru launched EcoCare's Meta Ads program and grew it into a dependable lead engine for home-comfort services — from 32 lead-form enquiries in the first month (Aug 2025) to roughly 396 genuine leads a month in the most recent complete quarter (Apr–Jun 2026), at about $26 per lead across furnaces, heat pumps and water heaters.

### About the Client
EcoCare is a home-comfort and HVAC services provider serving homeowners across Ontario, Canada, including the Greater Toronto Area and Ottawa. The business installs and services furnaces, heat pumps, air conditioners, tankless and traditional water heaters, water treatment systems and EV chargers. Demand for these services is high-intent and often seasonal, and homeowners increasingly research and enquire on Facebook and Instagram. Meta Ads lets EcoCare reach those homeowners with the right offer at the right time and capture their contact details directly through lead forms, which makes reliable lead measurement central to the account's growth.

### How the Account Came to PPC Guru
EcoCare partnered with PPC Guru to build a Meta Ads lead-generation program from the ground up. Rather than a takeover of an existing account, this was a launch: PPC Guru stood up the campaign structure, lead forms and tracking, then grew spend in step with proven results. The first full month of delivery was August 2025, and the program has run continuously since.

### The Challenge
EcoCare needed a predictable flow of new-customer enquiries for a wide range of home-comfort services, in a competitive Ontario market and against seasonal demand swings. Starting from a new Meta Ads program meant proving, service by service, that Facebook and Instagram could deliver genuine leads at a cost that made sense before spend could safely be scaled.

### What We Analyzed & Planned
The plan treated Meta as a direct lead channel rather than an awareness play. Early testing in the first weeks compared messaging and engagement approaches against lead forms; lead forms won on both volume and cost, so budget was concentrated there. From there the account was built service by service — water heaters, furnaces and heat pumps, air conditioners, water treatment and EV chargers — so each offer could be measured on its own cost per lead and scaled independently.

- Launch Instant Form lead campaigns so homeowner enquiries are captured directly in-platform.
- Split campaigns by service line (water heaters, furnaces/heat pumps, AC, water treatment, EV chargers) to measure cost per lead per offer.
- Use Campaign Budget Optimization so spend flows to the best-performing service and audience automatically.
- Scale budget only as cost per lead held steady, month over month.

### Why Splitting Campaigns by Service Line Mattered
Rather than running one broad HVAC campaign, PPC Guru built separate campaigns for water heaters, furnaces and heat pumps, air conditioners, water treatment and EV chargers. Each service has its own audience, seasonality and cost per lead, so measuring them separately let the account pour budget into whatever was performing that month and pull back on what was not. Water heaters and furnaces/heat pumps became the workhorses, together producing the majority of genuine leads, while offers like EV chargers filled demand gaps. That structure is what let spend scale roughly 17× without cost per lead running away.

### Lessons for Home-Services Businesses
1. Use in-platform lead forms to capture enquiries directly — every extra click to a landing page costs you leads.
2. Split campaigns by service line so you can measure and scale each offer on its own cost per lead.
3. Test messaging, engagement and lead objectives early, then concentrate budget on whatever produces genuine enquiries cheapest.
4. Scale budget only when cost per lead holds steady, so growth adds real leads rather than just impressions.
5. Keep a retargeting layer running to recover people who opened a form but did not submit.
